forge · logo directions ii

Fourteen marks, drawn from what current developer tools and technical products actually put on a tab rather than from a guess. Every mark is one <svg viewBox="0 0 24 24"> in currentColor, shown at 96, 24 and 16px in accent and in single colour, so a direction that only works large fails in front of you rather than in a favicon nobody rechecks.

No gradients, no two-tone, no container that exists only to hold a gradient. A mark that needs two colours breaks the theme; a mark that needs three strokes is not a favicon.

The marks 14 directions, six families: solid primitive, many-of-one, fan and stream, container, monoline, letterform

01Wedgesolid primitivewedge

One solid mass with one straight edge and one curved: the corner of a circle that has been cut off. Nothing inside it to lose, which is the whole point of the family Vercel, Biome and Alacritty are in.

accent
white

Cannot fail at 16px: it is one silhouette with no interior edge, and the arc is 16 units of radius so the curve still reads as a curve in 10 pixels of ink. Light and dark both take it with no variant.

02Keystonesolid primitivekeystone

A solid trapezoid, narrow at the top. The piece that locks an arch, stated as pure geometry with no picture in it. The triangle's cousin without being the triangle everyone already has.

accent
white

The 6-unit top edge is 4px at 16px, so the taper survives. Sharp corners on the vocabulary from the print-engineering marks, and one fill with no interior line means nothing to mush.

03Gussetsolid primitivegusset

A thick L with a 45-degree brace across its inner corner. The bracket that holds a piece square, and the only mark here whose subject is the corner rather than the object.

accent
white

Both arms are 4 units, so at 16px it is a 2.7px bracket with a 2.7px diagonal, and the diagonal is the whole read. It sits in the same space as a code bracket, which is either a fit or a collision depending on how you feel about it.

04Railsmany-of-onerails

Four equal bars stacked tight. The Railway idiom, and the most literal statement of the product: many of the same thing running side by side, none of them ranked.

accent
white

Bar 3.2 units, gap 1.6: at 16px that is 2.1px of ink and a 1.1px gap, so the four bars survive as four and not as a slab. Four rather than three is what keeps it off clamp, which is already three bars on this sheet.

05Queuemany-of-onequeue

Three bars, each shorter than the last, all hung off the same edge. Work draining rather than work listed, which is the one thing a roster view cannot show at a glance.

accent
white

The honest risk is the icon dictionary: three descending bars left-aligned is what a signal-strength or a sort glyph looks like. The 8-unit shortest bar is what tips it, and it may not be enough. It is here because the idea is good, not because the drawing is finished.

06Panesmany-of-onepanes

A frame split into two uneven panes with the left one solid. The tmux mark, redrawn: sessions inside one window, and exactly one of them alive. It is the only mark here that shows containment and state at once.

accent
white

Four elements: the frame, the divider, the filled pane. At 16px it reads as a box with the left half dark, which is a shape difference rather than a colour one, so a flatten to one colour leaves it intact. The 2-unit gap between the pane and the divider is 1.3px, which is where it is tightest.

07Fanfan and streamfan

Three spokes swinging out of one corner. The lead-and-workers shape as a radial, which is the device the whole agent category has settled on this year, drawn from a corner rather than a centre so it is not the Claude burst.

accent
white

At 96px it is a fan. At 24px and below the 45-degree spoke wins and it reads as a diagonal arrow with a bar behind it, which is a different mark. The other risk is adjacency rather than size: this is a relative of the Claude burst, and forge is the thing that hosts Claude sessions.

08Feedfan and streamfeed

One diagonal, broken in the middle. Two parallel bars pointing the same way with a gap between them: a stream crossing a boundary, which is what a session stream is. It is also the two slashes a developer already reads as a comment.

accent
white

Two bars 4.4 units wide and 11.9 long with a 2.6-unit gap: at 16px that is 2.9px of ink, a 7.9px stroke and a 1.7px break. The break is the tightest number on this sheet, and if it closes the mark becomes a single diagonal. It is also a cousin of strike: same 45 degrees, different count, no crossing.

09Dockcontainer plus symboldock

A frame with a solid tile dropped onto its corner. The project holding a session, or the server holding a view. Two masses and no fine detail, which is the shape a favicon wants and the shape the whole app-icon convention is built on.

accent
white

Frame stroke 2.6, tile 7 units: at 16px a 10px box with a 1.7px outline and a 4.7px block clipping its corner, and the two stay two because the block is smaller than the hole it leaves. Its failure mode is the dictionary, not the size: a small solid mark on the outside of a ring at tab size is what a magnifier looks like. Moving the block to the middle of an edge instead kills the magnifier and introduces a battery, so this is the better of the two.

10Loopmonolineloop

A rounded square that almost closes, with the two ends left round and apart. The agent loop as one stroke, and the only mark here whose subject is the cycle rather than the contents.

accent
white

Stroke 3 gives 2px at 16px, which is the thinnest thing that survives here, and round caps keep the ends from looking cut. The 6-unit opening is 4px at 16px, so it reads as open. This is the mark that spins cleanly if the working state ever wants a motion variant.

11Dialmonolinedial

An open ring with a solid point at the centre. One live thing inside a cycle that has a way out, rather than a closed circle with nothing in it. OpenAI's rebrand reduced its whole mark to a solid point; this is that point with something around it.

accent
white

The centre dot is 5 units across and the ring sits at 8.4, so at 16px you get a 3.3px point inside a 2px ring with 2.9px of clearance. Both survive. The 55-degree opening is at the top-left, which is where a spinner's head would be, so this is also the one that animates most obviously.

12Orbitmonolineorbit

A closed ring with one solid bead riding it. The other end of the ring family from dial: here the loop is whole and the interest is the one thing on it. Reads as a session attached to a project that keeps going.

accent
white

A 2.6-unit ring is 1.7px at 16px, and the bead is 6.2 units so it survives, but the bead grows toward the ring as the render shrinks and the two start to merge around 20px. The cleaner version would thicken the ring to 3 and drop the bead to 2.6.

13Prismdimensional, flatprism

Three faces of a cube, separated by gaps rather than by shading, so it stays one colour. Cursor's isometric lineage, minus the shading that would break it on a dark page. Work shown from outside rather than in plan.

accent
white

The seams are 1.4 units, which is 0.9px at 16px: below 24px the three faces fuse into one hexagonal lump and the whole idea goes. Cursor keeps a separately drawn flat one-colour version as its primary mark for exactly this reason, so this direction means shipping two drawings, not one.

14F Monoletterformf_mono

The letter as one uniform stroke rather than as slabs or as a knockout. Resend and Anthropic both draw their letter as a path instead of setting it in a font; this is that construction at the lightest weight on this sheet.

accent
white

3.2 units of stroke is 2.1px at 16px and the gaps in the letter are 4.6 units, so an F still reads in a tab. It floats with no container, which means it has no tile to hold it on a light bar: on light it wants the near-black colour and nothing else.

The tab is the size that decides it. On a dark browser the mark takes the page's text colour; on a light one it wants the near-black fill, so the favicon ships as an SVG with a prefers-color-scheme block rather than as one flat file, and Safari needs a PNG fallback because it ignores that block on favicons.

What the vocabulary actually is read off shipped marks, not off taste

One mass, no interior detail
Vercel is a single triangle: m12 1.608 12 20.784H0Z, one path, no second element. Biome is one filled path. Alacritty is one path of nested chevrons.
The recurring argument is that extreme constraint buys recognition: the mark is describable in one sentence and drawable in one shape.
Many of one thing, stacked
Railway is horizontal rail bands. Warp is two offset blocks with a notch. tmux is literally a divided rectangle. Devin is seven rounded hexagons arranged into one larger hexagon, per its registered trademark text.
This is the closest family to what forge is: the product is many-of-one-thing, and the category draws that directly.
Monoline with rounded joins
Sentry is nested chevrons in one uniform stroke. Raycast is a rounded frame with a chamfered inner square and corner ticks. Ghostty is a single filled terminal with the glyph knocked out of it.
The trap in this family is stroke weight: anything under about 2.6 units on a 24 grid rasterises away at 16px.
The letter drawn as a path
Resend is an R cut as one path, not set in a typeface. Anthropic is a filled A with the counter as a subpath. Linear is four arc strokes and reads as a letterform without being one.
A letter survives being 10px wide in a tab in a way no abstraction does. The cost is that it is the safe answer, and the category is full of them.
Negative space inside a field
Claude's app icon inverts: white burst on a solid orange tile. Third-party harnesses now carry Claude's burst, Amp's three-bar red mark and others as standardised SVGs so agents keep an identity inside someone else's UI.
A tile gives 16px a solid edge to hold, which is why klin still holds up better than most of the original sheet.
45-degree, and optical correction
Cursor's identity writeup is explicit that the original cube was mathematically perfect isometry that would not render at small sizes, so the corners were rounded and the cursor offset to open a gap: abandoning geometric purity for optical truth. A separate flat one-colour version is the primary mark; the 2.5D and 3D versions are for the app icon.
Read as: a mark that needs its shading to work is not the primary mark.

Out of fashion, and left off this sheet: gradients as the mark itself (the soft AI-default blob is now called a liability rather than a shortcut), glassmorphism applied without purpose, glossy 3D skeuomorphic containers, thin-stroke line icons at small sizes, and anything needing three strokes or two colours to be legible. Also out: a mark that only exists large and gets redrawn smaller - one drawing has to do the whole range.

What I would pick and what is only different

01Improvement: panes

The only mark on either sheet showing containment and state in one drawing, and it does it with a shape difference rather than a colour one, so flattening to one colour leaves the reading intact. It is the most product-specific thing here - forge is what puts several sessions in one window - and it is the only one with somewhere to go: the filled pane can become the live-session count, so the tab itself reports how much is running without a second mark.

02Improvement: rails

Four bars at 2.1px with a 1.1px gap. The plainest possible statement of the product, surviving every size and both backgrounds with nothing added, and the safest mark on either sheet. It is the honest successor to lanes: four equal bars read as a fleet where three unequal bars read as a chart. The cost is that it is quiet to the point of being generic.

03Improvement: dock

Two masses, one outline and one solid: a 1.7px stroke and a 4.7px block at 16px. It is the best looking mark on either sheet and it is built from the shape the category actually uses for an app icon. Its failure mode is the dictionary rather than the size - a small solid block outside a ring at tab size is what a magnifier looks like - which is why it is third and not first.

04Improvement: gusset

A bracket that is a brace rather than a bracket. 45 degrees is the deliberate angle in this category right now, both arms are 4 units so nothing thins out, and it is the only mark here whose subject is a corner. Same class of risk as dock: it sits where a code bracket sits.

CUTPrism, orbit, queue

Prism needs two drawings to work, the definition of the thing this sheet avoids. Orbit's bead and ring merge below about 20px. Queue has the best idea and the most fragile drawing - three descending bars is a signal-strength glyph at 16px - so it is worth one more pass, not a place in the top three.

-Just different, not better

wedge, keystone, feed, fan, dial, loop, f_mono. Every one is defensible and every one is a taste call rather than a legibility win, so none of them belongs in a top four. fan is the sharpest case: a real improvement in category fit, and a real adjacency risk, since forge hosts Claude sessions and the Claude mark is a radial burst. On the evidence of the tab strip above, the ones worth a second look are loop and f_mono, which both hold at 16px better than the rest of this group.

What already fits the first sheet, read against what the category does

KEEPKlin, Lanes, Chamfer

These three already sit in the modern vocabulary and I would not replace them. Klin is a negative-space knockout in a solid tile, exactly the shape the app-icon convention is built on, with a 6px arch at 16px. Lanes is the many-of-one direction done with a rhythm rather than a grid. Chamfer is the 45-degree cut: one shape, nothing to mush. If Klin stays the default, nothing here has to move.

-Fine, but not the strongest in their family

F Slab and Stencil F are both letterforms, a live direction, drawn better than most - but that is one family represented twice on a sixteen-mark sheet, which is more than the vocabulary supports. Strike is the strongest pure geometry on the first sheet and its only real problem is the X-equals-close read. Nest and Grid are competent; Grid's problem is the dictionary, not legibility.

DROPCascade, Slab, Spark, Anvil

Cascade's gaps close at 16px and the rhythm that is the whole idea disappears. Slab's three isometric plates merge into a striped lozenge, and the fix is two plates, which is a different mark. Spark and Anvil are both the literal cliche of the name: a spark says nothing a hundred other sparks do not, and the anvil's three openings are 2.1px bands at 16px so the shape stops being an anvil. The most-cited rebrands this year are explicit that literal depictions of the product's subject are the thing being avoided.

-Honest summary

Of the sixteen on the first sheet, three already fit the current vocabulary well, four are drawn competently but sit in families the sheet covers elsewhere, and four are the literal cliche or a small-size failure. That is a good hit rate for a sheet drawn from one person's head. This second sheet is not a rebrand of that one: it is the same job with the vocabulary read off shipped marks first, and of its fourteen, eight are a legibility win while six are a taste call.

Implementation notes for whichever survives

Every mark is one <svg viewBox="0 0 24 24"> using currentColor, so the accent is a color change on the caller and nothing else. The shapes live in one <defs> block at the top of this page and every size is a <use>, so a drawing exists once and a change lands at all three sizes at once. In the crate the same string goes into mark_path in crates/forge-web/src/brand.rs, and every_shipped_name_has_its_own_drawing fails the build for any name added to MARK_NAMES without a drawing of its own beside it.

None of this changes anything in the product. It is a sheet to pick from or reject.
