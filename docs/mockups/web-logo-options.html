forge · logo directions

Sixteen marks, drawn as inline SVG in the brand palette. Each one is a standalone mark, not a wordmark: it has to carry a browser tab on its own. Every mark is shown at 64, 32, 24 and 16px, in accent orange and in single colour, so a direction that only works large fails in front of you rather than in a favicon nobody rechecks.

The directions 16 marks, four families: concept, geometry, letterform, negative space

01Lanesconcept

Three bars, bottom-aligned, every one a different height: parallel sessions in flight, none of them finished. The mark is the roster at a glance.

accent
white

The safest small mark here. Four units of bar on a 24 grid is 2.7px of ink at 16px, and the differing heights survive as rhythm rather than detail.

02Splitconcept

One block at the top, a stem, a spread bar, three legs. A lead agent and the workers it spawns, drawn as a plain org tree.

accent
white

Reads as a tree at every size, but it is the busiest of the concept marks: six shapes is a lot to hold at 16px, and the stem between the block and the bar fills in.

03Spineconcept

A session tree seen from the side: one spine, two branches, two nodes hanging off it. The branch is the spawn, the node is the worker.

accent
white

The horizontal branches are 3 units thin, which is under 2px at 16px, but they only have to connect two masses, so the 16px form still reads as a spine with two things on it.

04Slabconcept

Three plates stacked in isometric: work queued behind work. The only mark in the set that implies depth rather than a flat plane.

accent
white

At 16px the three plates merge into a single striped lozenge. It is the one mark here whose small form is honestly worse than its large form, and there is no way to fix that without dropping to two plates.

05Gridconcept

Four cells, three of them outlines and one solid: the fleet, with exactly one thing running. The solid cell is the mark's whole payload, and it is a shape difference rather than a colour one, so a flatten to one colour leaves it intact.

accent
white

The risk is not small size, it is the icon dictionary: four tiles is what a "dashboard" or "apps" glyph looks like everywhere else, and the one solid tile is doing all the work of separating it from that.

06Clampconcept

Two jaws and the billet between them. Work held while it is being worked on, and the most horizontal of the marks: three bands, no detail to lose.

accent
white

Widest silhouette in the set, so it owns a tab. Its failure mode is the opposite of mush: at 16px it is a stack of three bars, which is close to how a menu or a list glyph reads.

07Strikegeometry

Two bars crossed: the mark a smith stamps into the work. Drawn as two rounded pills at 45 degrees so the union is one solid mass with no notch at the crossing.

accent
white

The strongest geometry in the set and the cleanest at 16px, but an X is also the close button. The pill ends and the thick 4.2-unit bars are what push it toward a stamp rather than a control, and it still may not be enough.

08Cascadegeometry

Three chevrons falling in step: a run of strikes, or a run of delegations, each one landing under the last.

accent
white

This is the mark I would cut. The 1.2-unit gaps between chevrons are 0.8px at 16px, so the three bands close up into one thick wedge, and it loses the rhythm that is the entire idea.

09Nestgeometry

A ring inside a ring. Projects holding sessions holding work: the same shape at three scales, which is what the hierarchy actually looks like.

accent
white

The inner hole closes to about 2.5px at 16px, so the small form is a dark pip in a ring. That is deliberate: the ring carries it, the hole is the reward for looking closer.

10Chamfergeometry

A billet with one corner taken off. The smallest possible statement of "this material has been worked": one flat against three rounds.

accent
white

One shape, no interior detail, so nothing can mush. At 16px it is a tile with a clipped corner: quiet, and the sort of mark that only becomes yours after people see it a hundred times.

11Tallyconcept

Four strokes and a fifth across them: work counted rather than described. A workshop ledger, and the only mark here whose subject is quantity.

accent
white

The idea is the best of the sixteen and the execution is the most fragile. Five strokes in a 24 grid means the small form is a comb with a slash through it, and it needs the strokes at 3 units with the diagonal re-cut to hold.

12Klinnegative space

A kiln door: a solid tile with an arched opening cut out of it. The workshop the name comes from, stated as the hole rather than the object.

accent
white

The walls are 4.5 units and the opening is 9 wide by 13 tall, so at 16px you get a 6px arch in a 12px tile with nothing left to lose. Light variant needed: the tile is the ink, so on a light page it wants the near-black fill rather than the accent.

13Stencil Fletterform

The letter as a hole. A solid tile with an F cut out of it, drawn as one path with the openings as subpaths, so it is the same shape the whole way down.

accent
white

The F strokes are 3.5 units, so at 16px they are 2.3px openings: the letter is still readable in a tab, which is unusual for a negative-space mark. Light variant needed, same reason as Klin.

14F Slabletterform

The same letter in positive: an F built from three slabs of equal weight, terminals squared. A plate letter rather than a font letter, so it reads as something stamped.

accent
white

The one mark that needs no explanation in a tab: a letter survives being 10px wide in a way no abstraction does. It is also the only direction here that is already solved elsewhere, which is the argument against it.

15Anvilliteral

The cliche, drawn as a knockout because that is the only version of it that survives: a solid tile with the anvil cut away, so 16px loses the shape of the anvil, not the mark.

accent
white

At 16px the three openings are 2.1px bands, so the anvil stops being an anvil and the tile becomes a stack of stripes. It is the best anvil I could draw and it still does not earn the cliche. Light variant needed.

16Sparkliteral

The other cliche: a four-point spark, drawn as one path with concave sides so it is a single silhouette rather than four strokes meeting at a point.

accent
white

The waist is 4.8 units from the centre on a 10.5-unit arm, so the arms stay fat enough to read at 16px as a diamond with bitten sides. It is a spark that works and still says nothing about forge that a hundred other sparks do not.

What I would pick and what I would cut

01Klin

It is the only mark that is both a solid tile and a picture, which is exactly the shape a favicon wants: at 16px it is 12px of ink with a 6px arch in it, and at 64px it is a kiln door. It carries the name without spelling it, it is a single path, and it is the same drawing at every size. It wants a light variant, which is one line of CSS.

02Lanes

The most honest mark in the set: three sessions at three heights is what the product actually is, and it is the best performer at 16px. It also survives flattening and light backgrounds with nothing added. The cost is that it is abstract enough to need the name beside it for a while, which is fine for a tool that will only ever be seen by the person using it.

03F Slab

The fallback if the brand needs to be read rather than recognised. A letter holds a 16px tab better than any abstraction here, the slab construction keeps it from looking like a borrowed typeface, and it doubles as the app icon. The argument against it is the one that matters: it is the safe answer, not the interesting one.

CUTCascade

I would drop it. The 1.2-unit gaps between the three chevrons are 0.8px at 16px, so the bands close up and the rhythm that is the whole idea disappears: at the size the mark has to work at, it is a wedge. Fixing it means dropping to two chevrons, which is a different mark, and Lanes already covers "several things in flight" better. Runner-up for the cut is Tally, which has the best idea here and the most fragile drawing: it is worth one more pass before it is judged.

Implementation notes for whichever survives

Every mark above is one inline <svg viewBox="0 0 24 24"> using currentColor, so switching between accent and single colour is a color change on the element and nothing else. The favicon set comes out of the same geometry: render the 24 grid at 16, 32 and 48, and add a light-mode <link rel="icon" media="(prefers-color-scheme: light)"> pointing at the same mark with a dark fill for the two knockout marks. Nothing needs a stroke scaled for size, and no mark depends on two colours to be read.
